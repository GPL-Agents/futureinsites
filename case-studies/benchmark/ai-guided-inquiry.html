Benchmark Senior Living, AI marketing recommendation

One inquiry. Two very different decisions.

A progressive inquiry path that begins with who is searching, adapts the conversation to the resident or adult child, and steadily improves lead scoring without turning the first visit into an intake interview.

16 signals

The model predicts who searches, drives, and decides, the likelihood to convert, and the likely care level. The inquiry path gathers only the highest value signals first.

The design question

Ask less now. Learn more over time.

Benchmark already supports the core outcomes people seek: pricing or a brochure, an advisor call, a tour, and other help. The gap is orchestration. A resident planning a lifestyle change and an adult child responding to a safety concern should not receive the same sequence, proof, or follow-up.

Observed on the Waltham pageThe standard information form requires first name, last name, email, phone, and an inquiry type. The embedded pricing chat widget begins by asking whether the visitor is searching for “Myself” or “My Loved One,” which captures a valuable role signal. However, both answers continue through essentially the same question path. The redesign uses that first answer to create distinct resident and adult-child conversations.
Prospective residentLead with autonomy, lifestyle continuity, peer proof, and practical fit.

Synchronized data strategy

One profile, enriched across every touchpoint

Each answer becomes a timestamped signal on one lead record. The scoring service recalculates after the web inquiry, advisor call, tour, and follow-up email, while preserving the source of each answer and the model’s confidence.

01, ONLINE

Earn the next interaction

Collect four strong signals before contact details, then ask for email so the conversation can be saved.

  • Relationship to resident
  • What help is wanted
  • Primary trigger
  • Move timeline
02, HUMAN FOLLOW-UP

Resolve sensitive ambiguity

A counselor confirms needs instead of asking the visitor to diagnose a care level.

  • Decision participants and approval
  • Family agreement
  • Budget and location fit
  • Daily living and memory needs
03, BEHAVIOR

Let actions update readiness

Observed engagement is often more reliable than another form field.

  • Guide downloaded
  • Call completed
  • Tour requested or completed
  • Email response and stated changes

Model governance

Five safeguards built into implementation.

The scoring model guides marketing follow-up and prioritizes what to learn next. It does not make admissions, pricing, eligibility, or care decisions.

1. Confidence controls sparse predictions

Unknown answers remain neutral, coverage is shown separately, and classifications below the certainty threshold are suppressed or clearly labeled.

2. Overlapping role signals are monitored

Relationship to the resident and who initiated the search answer distinct operating questions. Calibration prevents those correlated signals from creating overconfidence.

3. Care interest supports routing, not eligibility

Care preferences help select the right journey and information. They do not determine eligibility, and correlation monitoring prevents them from duplicating relationship and trigger signals.

4. Pricing and distance settings are community specific

Each community configures ranges by care level, local pricing, catchment area, availability, and observed outcomes so the same model reflects local market conditions.

5. Outcome calibration is continuous

Weights and answer points are back-tested against inquiry, tour, deposit, move-in, lost-lead reason, and time-to-conversion data, with performance monitored by audience and protected class proxies.
