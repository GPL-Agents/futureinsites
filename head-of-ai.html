Approach

Applying AI to solve real business problems.

Six principles I apply to every AI platform, whether I am starting from zero or inheriting something already in production.

01
Where AI Should and Should Not Be Used

My first question is whether AI is the best solution to the problem. A chatbot with predictable questions may work better as a decision tree, and a model earns its place only where simpler systems fall short. That discipline keeps AI investment tied to return.

02
Human-in-the-Loop Validation

Human review is matched to the risk of each task, and reviewer feedback is captured as the benchmark for correct output. As a system proves itself in production, we adjust the level of oversight based on measured reliability.

03
Model Routing, Fallbacks, and Token Economics

Each task is routed to the model that fits it, with fallbacks from LLM to rules to human review when something fails. Cost and latency budgets are set with engineering during design and tracked against production usage month over month.

04
Data Governance, Lineage and Privacy

Customer and business unit data stay separate, access is controlled by role, and explicit rules govern what data models can see, retain, or train on. These controls are built into the platform at the start, where they are enforceable.

05
Responsible AI and Guardrails

Every AI capability gets a risk tier before it ships, and the tier sets the depth of red-team testing, input and output filtering, and bias audits. Higher risk earns heavier guardrails and more human oversight, applied consistently across the platform.

06
Observability, Evaluation, and Continuous Learning

The systems we ship are instrumented end to end: every model call is logged with its cost, speed, and output quality, automated evaluations run on every change, and quality drift is caught early. Production results drive continuous improvement.
