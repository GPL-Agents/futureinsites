90-Second Career Narrative

Updated 90-Second Career Narrative

I focus on turning ambitious ideas into scalable products by aligning technology, data, and marketing, and by building the teams and operating models required to make those ideas work in practice.

I started in digital marketing, running paid search, affiliate, and lead generation programs. Early on, I became closely involved in how targeting, attribution, and performance systems should work—defining requirements, partnering with engineers, and driving improvements that made campaigns more effective.



I then founded FreeScholarshipGuide, where I built the business from the ground up—setting up operations, hiring and managing the team, running marketing, and defining product direction. While I didn’t write the platform myself, I led development end-to-end and built a scalable acquisition engine before the company was acquired.



After that, I moved into larger organizations. At Nelnet, I ran a performance marketing business supporting higher education clients, with P&L responsibility for a $35M revenue line and a 50+ person team. The work focused on driving student acquisition through multi-channel marketing, while building the analytics, lead management, and operational infrastructure needed to scale efficiently.



A key inflection point came at ownerIQ. I was initially brought in to assess and lead the managed service business, but after evaluating platform performance, I was asked to step into a product leadership role. At that point, I had limited experience with real-time bidding, but ramped quickly and led product across identity resolution, audience targeting, attribution, and expansion into mobile and video.



That work contributed to capabilities like offline purchase attribution and a retail media audience platform, which became core drivers of growth and part of the reason ownerIQ was acquired by Inmar. After the acquisition, I led the integration of ownerIQ’s platform with Inmar’s couponing, promotions, and customer data systems, bringing together media, identity, and transaction data into a unified platform.



Alongside that, I’ve been completing my MBA and focusing heavily on AI and machine learning—getting hands-on with Python, modeling, and how these systems translate into real products. That led me to MotiveMetrics, where I joined to help rebuild and scale an AI-driven paid search platform. There, I built and led product, engineering, and data science teams working on machine learning systems, LLM-based workflows, and platform integrations, while also improving reliability and cost efficiency across AWS workflows.



Across each stage of my career, the consistent pattern has been stepping into moments of change, learning quickly, and building the teams, platforms, and operating structures needed for the next phase of growth.

Leadership Lessons

I balance GTM speed with forward-looking design, shipping early to learn while avoiding dead ends and unnecessary rework for future feature iterations.

Scaling requires operational discipline, not just innovation.

AI products work best when human and machine roles are intentionally designed.

System-level improvements drive more impact than isolated fixes.

I take accountability for outcomes and ensure the team gets credit for success.

Feedback is direct and private; learning and progress are shared.

I lead with curiosity and assume I can learn something from everyone by asking the right questions and creating space for input.

My leadership approach is grounded in patience and empathy, which shapes how I support teams, develop individuals, and navigate challenges.
