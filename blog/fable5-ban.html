U.S. Government Orders Anthropic to Block Foreign Access to Claude Fable 5

The Commerce Department directed Anthropic to block all foreign nationals from accessing Fable 5, citing a jailbreak vulnerability. Anthropic pulled the model entirely rather than attempt nationality-based access controls it can't enforce at scale.

By Greg Loeffelholz

Claude Fable 5, launched just over a week ago, is now unavailable worldwide. (Image: Matteo Della Torre/NurPhoto via Getty Images)

If you fired up Claude over the weekend and saw Fable 5 missing from the model picker, you're not alone. Anthropic pulled Fable 5 after the U.S. Commerce Department issued an emergency export control directive on Friday, June 12 -- an order Anthropic says it cannot comply with without shutting the model down entirely.

The directive, first reported by Axios, requires Anthropic to block all foreign nationals from accessing Fable 5 -- whether inside or outside the United States, including foreign national Anthropic employees. Since the company lacks the technical infrastructure to enforce nationality-based access controls at scale, Anthropic chose to pull access for everyone rather than risk noncompliance.

What Happened

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sent a letter to Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei on Friday afternoon stating that Fable 5 would be subject to export controls to any location outside the U.S. According to Reuters, the government cited national security authorities and a reported "jailbreak" technique that bypassed Fable 5's safety guardrails.

Anthropic's public statement describes the alleged vulnerability as "a potential narrow, non-universal jailbreak, which essentially consists of asking the model to read a specific codebase and fix any software flaws." After reviewing the demonstration, Anthropic found that Fable 5 surfaced only a small number of known, minor vulnerabilities -- and that other publicly available models, including OpenAI's GPT-5.5, can also discover those same code issues without any special prompt techniques.

Fable 5 is the public version of Anthropic's Mythos-class architecture, the company's most capable model family. The full Mythos 5 had previously been restricted to vetted organizations through Anthropic's Project Glasswing program -- a separate decision that predates the government's action.

The Political Backdrop

This isn't a standalone event. In March 2026, WIRED reported that the Pentagon labeled Anthropic a "supply chain risk" after the company refused to enable Claude for domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons applications without safety restrictions. The Commerce order can be read as a new front in an ongoing dispute between the government and a company that has staked its brand on responsible AI deployment.

The move is among the most aggressive uses of export-control powers against a commercially deployed AI model, according to Forbes. It raises immediate questions about how the U.S. government intends to regulate frontier AI capabilities on an ongoing basis.

What Is a Jailbreak?

Taken from the concept of bypassing carrier restrictions on mobile phones, a jailbreak in AI refers to a technique that manipulates a model into bypassing its built-in safety guardrails. In this case, the government claims it found a method that could cause Fable 5 to generate cyber offense capabilities without the safeguards Anthropic had built in.

Anthropic disputes the severity. The company notes that before launch, Fable 5 was red-teamed for thousands of hours by the U.S. government, the U.K. AI Security Institute, and multiple third-party organizations -- none of which found a universal jailbreak. As CNET reported, Anthropic also pointed out that no model provider has yet achieved perfect jailbreak resistance, and that the industry-standard safeguards are vulnerable to non-universal jailbreaks by design.

Broader Industry Implications

Anthropic's warning to the industry is pointed: if this jailbreak standard were applied across the board, it would effectively halt all new frontier model deployments. This is a direct appeal to OpenAI, Google, and others to treat the Commerce order as a shared problem rather than a competitive opportunity.

The logic is straightforward. If a narrow, non-universal jailbreak -- one that doesn't work across all contexts -- is sufficient grounds for an emergency export control shutdown, then every new frontier model faces the same risk. Every model has edge cases where safety guardrails can be bypassed in specific circumstances. That's not a bug unique to Fable 5; it's a property of the current state of the art in AI safety.

As Quartz noted, Fable 5's safeguards were described by Anthropic as "substantially more effective than those of any previously deployed model." The model includes classifiers designed to block responses in high-risk areas such as cybersecurity -- the same safeguards the government's jailbreak technique reportedly circumvented.

What Happens Next

Fable 5 will remain down indefinitely. Anthropic has stated it does not currently have a method to reliably enforce nationality-based access controls at scale, so a simple "U.S. users only" switch isn't on the table.

Two likely scenarios going forward:

  • Litigation. Anthropic challenges the export control order in court. The company has the resources and incentive to fight, and a legal challenge would test the scope of the Commerce Department's authority over AI model deployments.
  • A technical compromise. Anthropic develops nationality-gating infrastructure -- possibly through IP geolocation, verified credential systems, or employer-based verification -- that satisfies the government's concerns while restoring tiered access for U.S.-based users and organizations.

Either path will take weeks or months. In the meantime, Claude users who had adopted Fable 5 for production workflows are falling back to Opus 4.5 or migrating to competing models entirely.
