How we measure ROI

We put a number on the work before we touch it.

We do not ask you to take AI's value on faith. Start with one recurring workflow and write down four things:

  • What an hour of that work costs.
  • How long one completed pass takes.
  • How often it has to be done again.
  • How many times it runs in a month.

Those four numbers give you a baseline: hours per month times the hourly cost. That is what the work costs you today, before anyone touches AI. Then we build a small prototype and run it beside the process your team already uses, measured on the same four numbers.

Worked example

A two-person office reclaims three hours a week, each, on a recurring workflow. Both people earn $85,000 a year.

At $85,000, an hour of their time costs about $41, based on 2,080 working hours a year.

Scale it to your situation. At the same $41 per hour: 3 hours a week saved is about $6,400 a year; 6 hours is about $12,750; 10 hours is about $21,250. The arithmetic is simple on purpose. If the time saved does not clearly beat the baseline, we say so and stop.

What we count, and what we do not. We count the time saved, in hours and dollars, because that is what we can stand behind. We do not put a number on what that reclaimed time could earn you next. The upside is real -- time returned to billable work, to customers, to the owner's own priorities -- but it is an outcome, not a promise, so we keep it qualitative.

Examples

What this looks like in your business

The right use of AI depends on how your business operates. These are examples of the kinds of problems we can help address. Every solution is shaped around the organization, its employees, and the tools it already uses.

Law office · Cross-department

Attorneys and staff are using AI without a firm policy

Many small firms already use AI to draft documents and summarize case files, often in consumer tools, with no clear rules on which client information is off‑limits.

We can write a practical AI use policy, set up a secure firm-approved assistant, and train attorneys and staff to use it well while protecting client confidentiality.

Accounting firm · Finance

Manual data cleanup is eating into advisory capacity

Staff spend hours moving and reconciling client data across disconnected systems, chasing documents, and answering routine client questions, leaving less time for advisory work.

We can build AI-assisted workflows that gather and organize client data, flag exceptions for review, and draft routine client communications for staff approval.

Dental practice · Operations

The front desk is buried in insurance verification

Checking eligibility and benefits before each appointment means phone calls, payer portals, and manual entry into the practice management system, often while the practice is short-staffed.

We can set up AI tools for eligibility verification, patient calls, and appointment reminders, connected to your practice management system, so staff can spend more time with patients.

Roofing company · Marketing

Leads go cold while crews are on the roof

Speed to lead wins roofing jobs, but calls get missed during the workday and follow-up depends on whoever has time.

We can configure the AI answering, lead routing, and follow-up features in your roofing CRM, or build an agent that does the same, so every inquiry gets a fast response and lands in your pipeline.

Auto repair shop · Operations

Service advisors cannot keep up with customer communication

Status texts, inspection results, estimate approvals, and declined-work follow-up all compete for service advisors' time, and the shop management system and its add-ons do not always work together.

We can connect the right AI add-ons to your shop management system and design workflows that keep customers updated and follow up on declined work automatically.

Financial advisory firm · Operations

Client meeting notes and documents pile up

Advisors want AI to summarize meetings and pull key details from client documents, but every output has to meet supervision and recordkeeping requirements.

We can set up a business LLM environment with approved data sources, logged prompts and outputs, and human review built in, so advisors save time and compliance keeps visibility.

General contractor · Strategy

Project managers use AI, but there is no plan

Many project managers already use general AI tools for reporting and documentation, and AI agents are on the horizon, but accuracy concerns and scattered tools make it hard to know where to invest.

We can assess where AI fits across estimating, reporting, document management, and contract administration, then pick a focused first project with a measurable payoff.
